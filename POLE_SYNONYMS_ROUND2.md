# Pole-word synonym pairs — round 2 (dark register)

Second adversarial tournament, covering only sublimate/crystallise and smother/howl. Brief rewritten around Kahran's picks from round 1 (vanish/pierce, vanish/scar, haunt/burn, choke/spit, wrinkle/blush, ache/pulse, remember/pulse): verbs, both poles carrying a shadow, and the round-1 balance rule inverted — a pair fails if either side is safe, pretty, or merely descriptive; it does not fail because a pole is unpleasant to press. Teams: **Flesh** (one body, two states) vs **Blade** (the world acting on you). Three rounds, then a judge.

## smother/howl

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **fester** | **bleed** | both | one wound sealed and rotting inward or open and running where it can be seen; kept-in against let-out rather than quiet against loud, fester is the exact word for 'godforsaken heavy doubt', and both sides stain |

**Put in front of Kahran first:** fester/bleed first: both teams fielded it, it is the most exact smother word anyone found, and its see-saw is kept-in against let-out, not quiet against loud. gag/spew second: the ugliest, most directional throat pair on either list. cower/snarl third: one animal, and cower is the only negative that carries shame and fear together.

## Verdict

The seated list splits almost evenly: Flesh takes sublimate/crystallise seven to three and Blade takes smother/howl six to three with fester/bleed shared, so ten Flesh, nine Blade, one both. Each generator won the axis its stance was built for. Flesh was right that a body can dissolve from inside (faint, seep, fray, starve, blanch) and that is where the sublimate pole finally got words that cost something instead of weather; Blade was right that suppression is mostly done to the mouth by something else (gag, muzzle, bury, drown, crush) and its howl words are the only ones sized for 'expand your insides till you burst'. Both teams' cores were the pairs conceded by round two; their round-three additions were mostly weaker. Flesh's spelling tricks (gasp/gash, shed/shred, crawl/claw, brace/break) put the see-saw on the button but let words in on look rather than meaning, and Blade's institutions (kneel, banish, shun, dread/damn, stalk/strike) reintroduced exactly the comfortable everyday readings they had just purged. The inverted balance rule clearly produced better pairs than the first tournament: nothing seated here is a physics demonstration, every pair has two costs, and the calibration register (both words dark, bodily, verbs, monosyllabic where possible) is met by all twenty without reusing any calibration pair.

## Notable rejects

crawl/claw (Flesh): the best-looking button on either list, but claw is escape, not utterance, so it draws grip/release; Flesh's own round-2 attack on trap/claw was correct and they fielded it anyway. shed/shred (Flesh): shed is letting go (grip/release again) and 'shredded' is a gym compliment. glaze/gouge (Flesh): glaze is a doughnut and a pot before it is a dying eye; the shadow is entirely in the caption. starve/gnaw (Blade): both hunger, one axis of appetite, and gnaw grinds where the fragments cut; starve/carve is the right home for starve. shroud/flay (Blade): a hide/expose see-saw, not dissolve/sharpen, and shroud is the pretty gothic press. wither/sever (Blade): wither is the fern you forgot and its natural opposite is bloom. banish/impale, shun/brand, kneel/spit, dread/damn, stalk/strike (Blade): the throne, village, church, god and hunter each hand the visitor a clean everyday reading (banish doubts, a logo, a proposal, 'damn!', a lucky strike), which is the comfortable press the inverted rule forbids; kneel/spit also leans on a calibration word. scatter/skewer and deaden/sting (Blade): a tasteful funeral and a kebab, a first-aid kit; scale wrong. seethe/erupt (Flesh): coherent, but seethe is anger held and the smother fragments are fear and shame, and seethe already leaks, so crush/erupt took the erupt seat. clench/split (Flesh): clench is grip, split is a bill and a breakup. squirm/lash: lash is the whip that suppresses as readily as the lashing out, so its direction is not instant. wince/groan and brace/break: too small and too plain respectively; break points both ways (breakdown is smother). strangle/scream (Blade, earlier): dropped correctly for repeating the label, though bellow only half fixes it.
