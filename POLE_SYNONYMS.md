# Pole-word synonym pairs

Output of a three-round adversarial tournament: **the Docent** (immediacy — plain, bodily, on-sight legible) vs **the Oracle** (charge — image, strangeness, poetry in the button), each proposing 10 pairs per axis, attacking the rival's list, and revising; then a judge selecting 10 per axis from both. Hard constraints: single lowercase words (≤10 chars), true opposites along the axis's specific meaning, pressable (a force you can lean into), matched to the actual fragments on each pole, neither pole more attractive than the other.

| 6 | **mist** | **blade** | docent | vapour you walk through versus the edge that cuts; blade is the crystallise fragments' own word, mist verbs (mist up) |

| 5 | **stand** | **sway** | docent | one body, fixed against the wind or given to it (dancing now on the morning wind); no heavy/light or calm/wild leak |
| 6 | **wade** | **swim** | both | one river, feet on the bottom or given to the current; wading is as pleasant as swimming so the pair is level |

**Ship first:** anchor/float, stand/sway and wade/swim: the first is pure physics and the fragment that arrives is exactly the thing promised; the second is the one body the visitor is standing in, fixed or given to the wind, with no leak into any neighbouring axis; the third is one river with both halves equally pleasant to press. This axis is where the Oracle won outright, mostly because the Docent's equivalents (root/float, anchor/sail, stay/stray, bind/drift) were fine but collided on shared words.

## echo/ignite

| # | first pole | second pole | source | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ache** | **pulse** | docent | the body remembering versus the body alive this second; ache carries enough hurt that pulse does not simply win |
| 2 | **trace** | **touch** | docent | the same fingertip on the faint line something left and on the thing present under your hand; touch is a hair close to the touchscreen but the image is strong |
| 3 | **haunt** | **burn** | oracle | the past refusing to leave against the present consuming itself, both dark so neither is the safe press; burn does bleed a little toward the crystallise fragment scars on his wrist burn |
| 4 | **remember** | **breathe** | docent | the most immediate pair on the axis: remembering only possible about then, breathing only possible now; breathe is the calmer, more attractive press and the fragments blaze, so rotate rather than ship first |
| 5 | **ember** | **flame** | docent | same fire, before and now; two nouns and the one pair both teams agree beats most verbs on the axis |
| 6 | **ash** | **blaze** | oracle | what a fire leaves after it is over versus the fire burning now: after/during rather than low/high, and ash is dark enough to balance blaze |
| 7 | **yearn** | **bask** | oracle | wanting what is behind you versus lying down in what is here and warm; give me back my memories and moments of grace both land |
| 8 | **sigh** | **gasp** | oracle | one breath in two tenses, let out over something over and taken in at something happening; perfectly balanced, though the surface reading is weary/startled before it is then/now |
| 9 | **wrinkle** | **blush** | docent | two things the same skin does, time already passed over it versus blood in the face this second; both slightly unflattering so level, and the oddest image on the list |
| 10 | **rust** | **shimmer** | docent | two states of one piece of metal, what time alone does versus the ignite fragments' own word (shimmer me brighter); shimmer is the prettier press but rust has its own patina |

**Ship first:** ache/pulse, trace/touch and haunt/burn: the only three pairs on this axis where both words are bodily, tense-locked and roughly equal in pull. The axis is abstract and every other candidate pays somewhere: remember/breathe tilts to breathe, ember/flame and ash/blaze are nouns and fire, sigh/gasp reads as mood first, wrinkle/blush and rust/shimmer are charged but need a half-second. Kahran should expect to test this axis at the wall more than the other three.

**Ship first:** swallow/burst, shrink/swell and bury/erupt: one gut, one body, one ground, each with two directions and neither side flattering, and in every case the arriving fragment is the button's own word (pit grows in stomach, walls caving in, till you burst). This is the best-built axis either team produced and the three top pairs were agreed by both.

## Verdict

Neither team won cleanly, and the reason is instructive: by round three the two methods had converged on the same generator, one material or one body in two states, and every pair I would ship first (anchor/float, swallow/burst, fade/sharpen, smudge/scratch, shrink/swell, bury/erupt, fog/etch, wade/swim) is a pair both teams ended up fielding or conceding. Counting sources across the forty selections, the Docent supplies slightly more (roughly nineteen to the Oracle's seventeen, with the rest shared), and the Docent owns the top-three picks on sublimate/crystallise and smother/howl, the two axes that have a body to point at. The Oracle wins grip/release outright, partly on merit (orbit/stray, gather/scatter, tether/drift, clasp/wave are genuinely better than bind/untie or hug/twirl) and partly because the Docent's equivalents collided on shared words under the no-repeat rule. Echo/ignite is split and is the weakest axis for both: it is the one abstract axis, and neither team found more than three pairs where both words are bodily, tense-locked and equal in pull. What each got right: the Docent was right that a button is leaned on, not read, that interface words (press, hold, play, return, now, blur) are poison in this specific piece, and that pole balance has to be checked at the wall rather than in the notebook. The Oracle was right that the Docent's rule is a floor and not a generator (five vapour nouns in ten costumes), that ugliness on the grip and smother sides (cling, cower, dread) is not balance but punishment, and that the best plain pairs are the ones that happen to be images drawn from the fragments themselves. The one systemic weakness across every submission is the reciprocal-kill logic: both teams kept pairs alive on the ground that the opponent fielded the same word (vanish/scar, ghost/scar, choke/spit, haunt/burn, then/now, remember/pulse, vanish/pierce), so shared mistakes survived three rounds of attack that any neutral reader would have removed in one. A related habit is tolerating noun pairs whenever the image was good enough (ghost/bone, ember/flame, mist/blade, dust/dagger, sepia/neon): I have kept the four that lean on sight and flagged them, but Kahran should treat every noun pair as second-tier for a hold-to-commit button.

## Notable rejects (seductive but wrong)

then/now: the plainest words on the axis and dead on a button; you cannot lean into then, and now is the most-used call-to-action word on the internet. rewind/play: genuinely the most graspable hinge on echo/ignite, but play is a control, and on an interactive piece a visitor holding play expects the piece to start; it is also the obviously fun press. sepia/neon: lovely, instantly legible as old/now, and two adjectives with no force in them; sepia is an Instagram filter, and you cannot hold a colour. miss/kiss: kiss is the giggle press and the pair is romance, not memory/presence; the Oracle withdrew it and the Docent should not have picked it up. mourn/wake: beside mourn, wake is a funeral, so both buttons sit on the memory side. revisit/arrive: correct in meaning and dead in voice, a meeting room beside an airport. blur/carve and blur/etch: blur is the exact thing the losing poem does on screen, so the button contradicts the gesture. vanish/scar and ghost/scar: vanish is absence (echo's territory), scar is a wound (smother's), and scar is a punishing press; ghost/bone does the same work without the wound. vanish/pierce: not a see-saw, the opposite of vanish is appear and the opposite of pierce is blunt. melt/forge: forge reads as a signature and as making, and two hot words leave the visitor guessing what the hinge is. dust/flint: flint is a spark on sight, which hands the crystallise pole to ignite. simmer/boil: same direction, more heat; a simmer is already a small boil. brew/thunder: the one quiet/loud pair that slipped through, exactly the trap the brief names. slink/strut: shame against pride is a display axis, and strutting is not letting anything out. hug/twirl: a twirl never lets go of the hand, and hug is the warm press. plant/dance: plant reads as the houseplant first and dance wins every time. rein/gallop: a beautiful image where the gallop is the joyride and the rein is a chore. grieve/thrill: correctly tense-locked but grieve is a punishing press, thrill the fun one, and grief drifts toward smother. linger/flash: linger is staying put, a grip word, and flash is a camera. carry/fling: carry is endurance and faintly virtuous, not suppression. stash/spill and brim/spill: spill is a mishap or gossip, so the howl pole arrives as clumsiness; flood does the job. Finally two pairs that are not wrong but lost to the no-repeat rule: bottle/burst and swallow/spit are both excellent, and if Kahran prefers them, the swap is swallow/burst and choke/spit out, bottle/burst and swallow/spit in.
